This course is an introduction to American History from the end of the Civil War to the

present. The United States was able to recover after the Civil War and become an industrial and

colonial power by the turn of the century. The United States then became involved in world

affairs and ultimately took part in World War I. Following more than a decade of isolation the

United States reentered world affairs and emerged as a superpower with the defeat of Germany

and Japan in 1945. A Cold War would be fought with the Soviet Union for the next forty-five

years. My objective is to introduce you to these important themes that will allow you to better

understand the country in which you live.

Academic Integrity Policy
Academic integrity is part of the foundation of an academic community. Any violation of the highest standards of academic honesty threatens the trust upon which an academic community is built and is conduct that violates its fundamental principles.

Academic dishonesty is a serious breach of the rules of proper academic conduct. The penalty for the first act of academic dishonesty will be a zero on the piece of work involved or an F in the course, at the discretion of the instructor in consultation with the academic dean as appropriate.

Plagiarism is a distinct form of academic dishonesty in which a person uses the words or ideas of another without proper acknowledgment. But the definition of plagiarism cannot be satisfactorily stated in a few words, and students are encouraged to consult the handbook approved by the English Department for ENG 101 and 102. Students also are encouraged to consult with faculty members if they wish further clarification. Faculty will endeavor to distinguish between intentional plagiarism and the misuse of sources due to poor attribution skills.
